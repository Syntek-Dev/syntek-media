# NONFICTION.md — media-teach, non-fiction mode

The families a non-fiction author learns media in, and the house sources each practises against.
A non-fiction piece makes claims and quotes its sources aloud, so a practice piece takes its
quotations from a cited source and makes no claim the lesson cannot stand behind.

## Paths and unit

- **Workspace:** the topic's folder (`SKILL.md`, 'Where a topic lives'), and a temporary folder
  outside the repository for any clip or render.
- **Unit:** one lesson. Its practice piece imitates one piece (a teaching clip's beat, a talk's
  script, a board row, an edit decision list or a post package) but never sits in
  `scripts/src/pieces/`, is never registered and never takes a number.
- **House sources:** the guides in `scripts/docs/reference/`, `production/docs/reference/`,
  `publishing/docs/reference/` and `brand/docs/reference/`; the toolkit's own help;
  `brand/src/voice/voice.md`, read as reference and never edited; the book's method standard and
  voice notes, where syntek-author's layers are present (under standards/), read and never edited.

## Additions to the steps

- **Step 2 — also record the family:** craft, tool, platform or teaching media.
- **Step 4 — also,** a teaching media lesson's primary source is the rule itself (a translation's
  own permission statement for audio and video, a publisher's terms for quoting aloud, a
  platform's own policy), read through `media-research` and dated. A lesson on a passage of
  scripture cites it in the translation the author names, with its reference, never from memory.
- **Step 5 — also,** a practice argument takes a question nobody in the work is answering, and
  invented speakers and organisations (for example Morgan Example as a guest), so the practice
  cannot leak into a piece's position.

## Domain rules

| Family | What is practised | House source |
|---|---|---|
| Craft | one idea per clip; an example before the abstraction; making a claim's kind audible; a quotation cut to the words the point needs | `scripts/docs/reference/writing-for-the-ear.md`; `scripts/docs/reference/storyboards-and-shot-lists.md` |
| Tool | the toolkit's presets, cuts and checks; `card.py` and the card layouts; captions and published transcripts; loudness | `toolkit/CONTEXT.md`; `publishing/docs/reference/captions.md`; `production/docs/reference/sound-and-loudness.md` |
| Platform | reading the platform data; a cut-down that stands alone and keeps a hedge; a post package; the AI label | `publishing/docs/reference/platform-specs.md`; `publishing/docs/reference/cut-downs.md`; `publishing/docs/reference/posting-and-the-log.md`; `publishing/docs/reference/ai-disclosure.md` |
| Teaching media | permission to read aloud or show a quotation or a translation; the credit a permission asks for; a recorded talk brought in as a piece; where the project makes podcasts, a guest's release | `production/docs/reference/rights-and-consent.md`; `production/docs/reference/recorded-pieces.md`; the podcast-episode guide, where the project makes podcasts |

- **Permission to print is not permission to read aloud or to show.** A lesson that quotes a
  translation says what its terms allow for audio and video, or marks it `VERIFY`.
- **Never practise on testimony or first-person material** entrusted to the author.
- **A cut-down never loses a hedge for rhythm,** in practice as in the real work.

## Examples

- **Mission (craft):** 'Say what the text says, how it is read and what I think, so a listener
  hears the difference.' First lesson: three invented lines, one of each kind, read aloud and
  timed.
- **Mission (teaching media):** 'Know what I may read aloud from a translation.' First lesson: a
  translation's own permission statement read through `media-research`, then an invented clip's
  credit line written to its terms.

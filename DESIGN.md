# DESIGN.md — syntek-media

**Last Updated**: 06/10/2026 **Version**: 0.4.0 **Maintained By**: Syntek Studio
**Language**: British English (en_GB)

The build contract for `syntek-media`, a Copier template that adds audio and video production to a repository, in three brand kinds:
`business` (a business's social video, recorded talks and explainers), `author-fiction` (book trailers, audiobook and author socials for a novelist) and `author-nonfiction` (talks, podcast, audiobook and author socials for a non-fiction author).
It is built to be applied on top of a project generated by its sibling `syntek-author`, whose conventions it copies exactly, and it follows the house Copier idiom of `syntek-base`.

This file is the template repository's own design record. It sits outside `template/`, so it is never rendered.
Where this file and the convention maps disagree, **this file wins**. A decision syntek-media inherits unchanged cites syntek-author's number — "(syntek-author D23)" — and binds in the wording syntek-author gives it; a departure is marked **(overrides syntek-author Dn)** with its reason.

**Baseline.** "syntek-author" in this file means its working tree as read on 03/10/2026: commit `0ab33c5` (the v0.1.0 release) plus the uncommitted v0.2.0 work its own DESIGN.md records as D39–D44 — the project-owned `00-project.md` seed, the business document families, workflows cited by full folder name, and no tooling that reads what Git ignores — with 51 skills. Each release since names its own baseline in `CHANGELOG.md`: 0.2.0 and 0.2.1 were checked against `9a65902` (syntek-author's v0.3.0 release, read on 04/10/2026), from which `syntek-author-names.txt` was refreshed at 0.2.0; 0.2.2 against the same commit, read again on 05/10/2026; and the 0.3.0 work found syntek-author still at that commit, with a clean working tree, on 05/10/2026.
That tree is still being edited. Every builder re-reads syntek-author's DESIGN.md, and the file it is copying, at build time, and records any drift that changes this contract in `CHANGELOG.md`.
The names media must never take are frozen at this baseline in `.github/scripts/syntek-author-names.txt` (D27); the over-author renders and `coexist-test.sh` snapshot whatever tree `--author` names, and fail when its names differ from that file.

Every production layer: `<layer>/{CONTEXT.md, CLAUDE.md}`, `docs/{pair}`, `docs/reference/{pair, guides…}`, `docs/project/{pair}`, `src/{pair, …}`, `workflows/{pair}` (the "You want to… | Procedure" table in `workflows/CLAUDE.md`), `workflows/local/{pair}`, and `workflows/NN-name/{CONTEXT.md, CLAUDE.md, STEPS.md, CHECKLIST.md}` (syntek-author Section 4.4, verbatim). ### 6.2 Script and transcript

`scripts/src/pieces/<piece>/script.md` — frontmatter `piece`, `version`, `approved` (DD/MM/YYYY or empty), `words`, `estimated_seconds`; H1 `# <Title> — script`; one H2 per beat, `## N. <Beat name> (target MM:SS)`, the target being **that beat's own duration**, never a running time, so the beats' targets sum to the piece's (ruling T1; `script time` compares every beat with its own target); one sentence per line:

```markdown
## 1. Hook (target 00:03)

VO: {brisk} The ferry is late again.
TEXT: Late again?

## 2. The tide decides (target 00:20)

ON: Every crossing waits for the tide, not the timetable.
SFX: gulls, low
ON: {pause 0.6} So the timetable is a promise the sea never signed.
```

A spoken line opens with an upper-case speaker tag and a colon (`VO`, `ON`, `HOST`, `GUEST`, `NARRATOR`, a character's name); `TEXT`, `SFX`, `MUSIC` and `NOTE` are cues, never spoken. Directions sit in braces at the start of a line or before a phrase; `{pause S}` adds seconds. The script holds no IPA (pronunciations live in `voice.md`) and no stage direction outside braces or cue lines. **Delivery, effects and music remain in the script:** `{whispering}`, `{shouting}`, `{laughing}` or another direction records the intended performance, and `speak plan` converts it to an audio tag for a supporting model. `SFX:` describes a sound effect, and `MUSIC:` describes the music wanted and where it enters, changes or stops; neither is spoken text. A separately generated effect or music track is approved, logged and cleared through the existing footage and rights registers, then placed in the edit decision list as `[[audio]]` with `role = "effect"` or `role = "music"`. Its fades, gain and ducking stay in that list, which both master routes use. Music generation and commercial use depend on the author's eligible plan and current licence; the generation details and licence evidence are retained, and no workflow promises that a platform will never claim, mute or remove a track. The script's directions, effects and music also enter the cue index below, so animation and audio can refer to the same instructions and timing anchors. A line is cited as `beat.line` (`2.1`), counting spoken lines only. An audiobook has no `script.md`: its plan is its chapter register (Section 6.7), its credits' words are written beside it, and chapter text is read from its source by `media.py audiobook text` (D23) and never copied into a tracked file.
`scripts/src/pieces/<piece>/transcript.md` (a recorded piece, D46) — the same format, with frontmatter `piece`, `source` (the recording's footage ID), `made` (`speech-to-text DD/MM/YYYY` or `by hand`), `approved`; H1 `# <Title> — transcript`; beat H2 `## N. <Beat name> (at HH:MM:SS.mmm)`, the beat's start on the recording; spoken lines only, as said (no braces), with a word the transcript cannot settle written `[unclear]` and flagged `VERIFY`.

### 6.3 Storyboard and shot list

`storyboard.md` — frontmatter `piece`, `version`, `approved`; H1 `# <Title> — storyboard`; table `| # | Beat | Time | Picture | Spoken | On screen | Sound | Vertical framing | Timing notes |` — `#` B01…; Beat = the script's beat number; Time `MM:SS–MM:SS`, estimated from the beat targets at M3 and, for a scene piece, re-timed from `<piece>.cues.json` once its voice is timed (`M4.cues`, Section 6.1); Spoken cites script lines as `beat.line` (`2.1–2.3`); Vertical framing `centre`, `crop x=<px>`, `pad` or `native` (a scene rendered at the vertical size itself, never cropped: pixel art cropped or scaled leaves its grid); Timing notes the author's notes from the stills and the preview render, `—` where there are none. A board written before the column existed has eight columns, and `cues` reads it as having no notes, and a hyphen in Time or Spoken as the en dash. **A re-time is not a revision for M3**: re-writing Time (and the shot list's Seconds) from the words, or adding a Timing note that only moves time, raises `version` and keeps `approved:` and M3, clearing `M4.cues`, `M4.stills` and M4, and the piece goes back through `production/workflows/09-time-the-voice/`; a note that replaces a shot or changes the words is a revision, which clears `approved:` and M3 as before and goes back through `scripts/workflows/03-storyboard-a-piece/`.
`shot-list.md` — no frontmatter; H1 `# <Title> — shot list`; table `| Shot | Board | Type | Source | Framing | Seconds | Status | Rights |` — Shot S01…; Type `camera · screen · stock · still · card · colour · generated · scene`; Source a footage ID (`F0007`), an asset path, a card under `production/src/cards/`, a colour `#RRGGBB`, the piece's scene `production/src/scenes/<piece>.scene.py` (Type `scene`: a board the code-animated scene draws, which makes the piece a scene piece, Section 6.1; every board of a scene piece is one, because `scene.py render` draws the whole master, and the brief's `ai_visuals` agrees, D25; a scene piece's other shots name only the images and text captures its scene shows, which `media.py real` indexes, never a video: recorded video inside a scene is deferred, Section 11, and a piece that shows it is a footage piece, assembled, D47) or `to shoot`; Status `needed · captured · logged · cleared`; Rights a rights-register ID or `—`.

**The cue index** (`production/src/scenes/<piece>.cues.json`, written through `-o`, D66). The maintainer approved the separate-list schema and its delivery, effect and music events on 06/10/2026:

```text
{piece, beats: […], boards: […], lines: […], words: […], events: […]}
```

- `piece` is the piece key. Every beat, board and spoken line has its ID and exact `start` and `end` seconds on the joined voice; beats retain their titles, boards retain their Spoken references and Timing notes, and lines reference zero-based indices into `words`. Each word appears once, retains its alignment fields (`word`, `start`, `end`, `score`, `segment`) and adds its script-line ID. The index does not duplicate the words inside boards or beats.
- `events` preserves the script's delivery directions, sound-effect instructions and music instructions as `delivery`, `sfx` and `music`. IDs are `e01`, `e02` onwards in script order; regenerating after instructions change requires reviewing the links. Each records `instruction`, `beat`, `source_line` (one-based within the script body, after frontmatter), `script_line` (spoken line ID, or null) and `anchor`. Delivery anchors name a zero-based `word`, its `start` or `end` edge and exact `time`; directions before a phrase anchor to its next word, and trailing directions to the preceding word's end. They carry no inferred sound duration.
- Each SFX or music event connects to exactly one existing `[[audio]]` row through optional `cue = "e02"` (confirmed by the maintainer on 06/10/2026). That row supplies its footage `source`, `role = "effect"` or `"music"`, placement, source range, gain, fades and ducking. Its event copies these as `source` and `mix`, so the edit decision list remains the source of the actual mix settings. Its audio `anchor` identifies the zero-based row and `voice_offset`; exactly one `vo:<piece>` row locates the joined voice. Event `start` and `end` are exact seconds relative to that voice, potentially negative for an earlier music entrance; `mix.master_at` retains the master placement, with `source_in` and `source_out` retaining the source range. Unlinked, duplicate or incompatible rows are findings, never guessed placements.
- Anchors connect events to the timed words, lines, beats or boards. The original instruction remains available even when a generation model does not support it. Directions never become spoken words or caption text. A tag's position records intended direction; it does not prove that the generated audio obeyed it or establish the duration of a laugh or other non-word sound. The author reviews the performance, and separately generated effects and music have their own approved placement and duration.
- Unmatched rows retain null times and are reported with exit 1. Unresolved event anchors or unapproved sound placements are reported rather than assigned invented times. All numeric times are finite. `cues` prints its findings and the re-timed storyboard for review, and never rewrites the storyboard. Exact timings remain in JSON; the printed storyboard uses `MM:SS–MM:SS`, rounding its start down and its end up to whole seconds so the displayed range contains its spoken words, with exact seconds printed separately (confirmed by the maintainer on 06/10/2026).
- The toolkit builds this index offline from tracked sources. It never generates voice, effects or music itself; paid generation remains an author-agreed production action, and template development and audit fixtures make no paid calls.

### 6.9 Captions

SRT is the master format; VTT is generated from it. House limits: **≤ 42 characters per line** for landscape and square deliverables, **≤ 32** for 9:16; **≤ 2 lines** per cue; **≤ 17 characters per second**; a cue lasts 1.0–7.0 s; ≥ 0.08 s between cues. Break at sentence or clause boundaries, never inside a name. A speaker change inside one cue opens each line with `- `. Non-speech sounds in square brackets, lower case, only when they matter. No audio tags or braced directions. Words as the script or transcript spells them, en_GB.
Naming: `<piece>.en-GB.srt` (timed to the master) · `<piece>--cNN.en-GB.srt` (timed to a cut) · `<piece>.<FID>.en-GB.srt` (timed to a recording, D46: `003-….F0001.en-GB.srt`) · a `.<platform>-<format>` qualifier (the key's dot and every underscore as hyphens, Section 6.16) before `.en-GB` when a deliverable needs its own line width (`003-why-the-ferry-runs-late--c01.youtube-short.en-GB.srt`) · a `.vtt` beside each `.srt`.
A generated voice: `captions from-segments` on its register (D22 route 1) or, once its words are timed, `captions from-words production/src/timing/<piece>.words.json` (route 4), each with `--deliverable` and with `--offset` the voice track's start on the master → the master-timed SRT, then `captions retime --in --out` per cut, as for any master-timed SRT.
A recorded piece: `captions align transcript.md <recording audio> --anchors` → the recording-timed SRT (`production/workflows/08-bring-in-a-recording/`) → `captions retime --edl` → the master-timed SRT (`production/workflows/03-assemble-the-master/`, or `publishing/workflows/01-plan-the-cut-downs/` where it is still missing), whose cue times `repurpose` uses for each cut's In and Out → `captions retime --in --out` per cut, or `extract-audio --in --out` and `captions align --lines` where the author sees drift. Every caption file is written with `-o` (Section 4.2).
Burn-in: an ASS file with `PlayResX`/`PlayResY` = output size; style from the `--caption-*` tokens; `Alignment=2`; `MarginV` = the preset's `safe_zone.bottom`, or 8% of the height where none is published (a house value); `MarginL`/`MarginR` = its side margins; the fonts folder passed as `fontsdir`.
**On a profile site** (D60) the `.vtt` is the sidecar, loaded by `<track kind="captions" srclang="en-GB">` from the same origin or with CORS, and never burned in; an embed keeps the captions uploaded with its `youtube.long` deliverable. **The published transcript** `<piece>[--cNN].transcript.en-GB.md` (beside the captions, tracked; one per distinct cut placed, the whole piece's without `--cNN`) is made with `captions transcript TEXT [--lines B.L-B.L] -o …` from the script or transcript, then described by hand where the picture carries what the words do not; one sentence per line (D30), a paragraph per beat, speaker names only where two or more people speak, a recorded piece's line 'As recorded on DD/MM/YYYY'. The words are what was said: the spelling and grammar companions may report on it and fix only a mis-transcription. A feed episode's `podcast:transcript` is its master-timed `.vtt`.

## 8. Applying syntek-media

**Over a syntek-author project (the intended path).** On a clean, committed branch of the project, `uvx copier copy --trust gh:Syntek-Dev/syntek-media .` (or `--data-file` an edited copy of an `examples/` file, with `--data SEED_EXAMPLES=false` for a project already in use). **Never pass `--overwrite`.** The ten shared files already exist and are skipped; any other existing path is a collision, and a non-interactive copy stops with "Interactive session required" before writing it — report it, it is a template bug; interactively Copier asks "Overwrite …?" with default yes, so answer no and report it. (An update gives no such stop: Section 10.) Review `git diff`, make the hand edits below, and commit, including `.copier-answers.syntek-media.yml`. There are no adoption scripts: every media folder is new, so no move has exactly one right destination, and loose media already in a repository is the author's to sort.
**Standalone.** `uvx copier copy --trust gh:Syntek-Dev/syntek-media my-channel` (or `"$(realpath ../syntek-media)"` with `--vcs-ref=HEAD` for a local clone; never a relative path). The shared files are written from media's own copies, and the project works without companions, as D4 describes.
**The hand edits after a copy over syntek-author** (printed by `_message_after_copy`): (1) `.claude/settings.json` — add the four `Bash(…)` allows, the nine ElevenLabs `ask` entries and, in `deny`, `Edit(**/generated/**)` and `Edit(**/renders/**)` of D13 (syntek-author's seed has none of them, so every toolkit call would prompt, no credit call would, and nothing would keep hand edits out of the output folders); (2) optionally, a row in `README.md`'s layout table and the four layers in root `CONTEXT.md`'s tree, and a line in `.claude/CLAUDE.md` naming `.claude/rules/syntek-media/` — optional because the rules load anyway; (3) nothing in `.gitignore` or `.mcp.json` (the ignore rules are nested; the server is user-scoped); (4) `git lfs install` once, if `brand/src/exports/large/` will be used — after it the repository-local `filter.lfs.required` the tripwire may have set is harmless, since git-lfs sets the same value; (5) run `python3 toolkit/media.py check --setup` (`brand/workflows/06-check-the-setup/`) and clear what it reports before the first credit-spending call.
**ElevenLabs setup.** The server is user-scoped, named `elevenlabs`, and **its base path must contain the project**, or speech-to-text rejects every project file ("outside of allowed directory"; the path is resolved before the test, so a symlink does not help). Use `$HOME` when every project lives under it; otherwise the nearest folder that holds them all, such as the disk the repositories live on. An existing server is removed and re-added:

```bash
claude mcp remove elevenlabs --scope user
claude mcp add --env ELEVENLABS_API_KEY="$ELEVENLABS_API_KEY" --env ELEVENLABS_MCP_BASE_PATH="$HOME" \
  --transport stdio --scope user elevenlabs -- uvx elevenlabs-mcp
```

Replace `"$HOME"` with that folder where projects live elsewhere; the `captions` skill prints the exact command, with a base path it has computed to contain `git rev-parse --show-toplevel`, when the error appears. Export the key in the shell first; never type it on the command line or commit it.
**Updating.** Each template updates separately from a clean tree, committing between: `uvx copier update --trust -a .copier-answers.syntek-media.yml`, review, commit; syntek-author's own update uses its own `-a`. A list answer passed on update replaces the whole list. `BRAND_KIND` never changes; a different kind is a new project. The MissingFileWarning about `.copier-answers.syntek-media.yml` is expected on a fresh copy and on every update (Copier's temporary renders read it too).

## 9. Coexistence rules

syntek-author Section 9, and how media keeps each rule:

1. **Each template sets its own `_answers_file`** — media's is `.copier-answers.syntek-media.yml` (D3).
2. **Each template's instructions live in `.claude/rules/<template>/`; no template updates `.claude/CLAUDE.md`** — media's eight files are in `.claude/rules/syntek-media/`, and the brand brief is rules 01 Section 1 (D5). syntek-author's project settings file, where present, outranks both templates' rules (D5).
3. **Shared root files are seed-if-missing** — media is stricter: its seventeen shared paths are copy-only, and it ships none of the `.claude/hooks/` pair (D11).
4. **Skill and layer names never collide; the shared writing skills ship only from `syntek-author`** — media's ten skills and five folders are new names, the companions are cited, never copied (D8, D10), and the frozen name list proves it on every run (D27). The 0.2.0 work adds no top-level folder, no `.claude/` path, no skill and no rules file: its new paths (`publishing/src/podcast/`, `publishing/workflows/08-publish-the-podcast-feed/`, three profiles and four guides) sit inside media's own layers, where the name list does not reach. 5. **Large binaries never sit in plain Git** — nested ignore rules for renders and generated audio; footage and approved takes external; LFS only for large design exports (D19, D52).

Media specifics: whichever template is applied first writes the shared files, so with syntek-author first they never mention media — rules 01, 02 and 04 carry everything instead. ## 10. Hazards

**Never template:** real brand or person names, channel handles, URLs and account IDs, ElevenLabs voice IDs, API keys, Claude Design links, follower or view counts, prices, and any binary (`template/` ships no audio, video, image or font). **Ship empty:** every seed, register, log and manifest.
**An update does not stop on a collision.** A copy stops on a template-owned path that already exists; an update does not: Copier forces overwrite on update, so a later media release that adds a path syntek-author renders exits 0 and leaves three-way conflict markers inside syntek-author's file (mechanics review probe `upd.sh`, Copier 9.18.2). Every new top-level folder, `.claude/` path, skill folder or rules file is checked against `syntek-author-names.txt` before release, `coexist-test.sh` must PASS (never SKIP) for a tag, and the name list is refreshed whenever syntek-author releases.
**Never delete a shared file's update-gated `_exclude` line**: without it the file becomes seed-if-missing, and a shared file the author deleted is recreated from media's copy on the next update — inside syntek-author's project. **Never delete its `_skip_if_exists` line**: without it a copy over an existing file aborts. An update renders the old release with the old release's `_exclude` as well as the new one, so a project's copy is kept when a release stops shipping the file (probe `gate.sh`: file and both lines dropped, the author's file kept); the file is still never removed or renamed in a later release (R5), because a renamed shared file is a second shared path neither template seeds.
**Never stop shipping a seed** without a migration that preserves it (probe h2: a filled seed was deleted silently). **Never add a root `.gitattributes`** or use `git lfs track`, which writes one: it would be a shared file neither template seeds. **The LFS tripwire writes the repository's `.git/config`** (D19): it is copy-only, never undone by media, and said in `_message_after_copy`. **Never negate a path back in under a gated directory** in `_exclude`, except the `README.md` of a README-only output folder (D42), whose negation is listed above every gated line, so a shut gate, matched last, still wins: Copier obeys the last pattern that matches, so a negation listed after a gate brings its path back with the gate shut (D19). **Never write `_external_data` anywhere but a validator** (probe c2c). **Never let the root `.gitignore` seed match a shipped path**: it applies to `template/` in this repository too, so the snapshot would drop the file. **Never name a tracked file with an extension syntek-author's root `.gitignore` ignores** (Section 6.16). **Never put a tracked file in a per-piece folder**: each sits inside an ignored output folder, so a file there is never committed and no session reads it (D50); a tracked file stays flat in its tracked folder under its `<piece>.` name (D64). **A text capture can carry a home path or a host name**, and `scrub.sh` never runs in an author's project: the assets pair has them removed before the capture is committed (D69). **Never name an example path in a template-owned file** (D43). **The companions' own procedures expect syntek-author's section drafts and write ledger entries**: media's rules 02 says to run improve-section and adapt-section as report-then-agreed-edit with no ledger step, and `write-script`'s revise step is the fallback when a companion refuses a non-section file. **The first `/design-sync` on a plain HTML kit is unproven** (`VERIFY`; D18). **Approved generated audio is unrepeatable**: a `git clean -fdx` or a lost disk destroys paid-for takes that were never archived (D52). **The ElevenLabs base path must contain the project**: `$HOME` does not cover a repository kept on another disk (Section 8). **syntek-author is still moving**: its working tree was mid-v0.2.0 at the baseline, so every builder re-reads it, and `coexist-test.sh` check 20 catches name drift.
**A show's feed URL and every GUID are for life** (D58, D59): a feed that moves needs a redirect and `new_feed_url` for at least `platform.podcast.feed_move_min_days`, and a GUID recomputed or retyped makes every directory list the episode twice; `feed new` and `feed add` write them once, `feed new --rekey` is refused once anything is published, and `feed write` and `feed check` refuse a GUID that vanished from the tracked feed. **The tracked feed is written only after the author reports the feed live**, and only by `feed write -o` after that comparison: a feed written first and checked after would compare itself with itself. **A corrected episode file gets a new URL**, never the old one overwritten: Spotify fetches a file again only when its path changes, and YouTube never replaces ingested audio; `feed write` and `feed check` refuse a changed length under an old URL, and `feed tag` on a published row says so. **One feed per show, on a site the brand owns**: another site may carry an episode page that plays the same audio, as a placement with its own agreement (D61), never a second feed. **The feed is uploaded last**, after every file it names, and at the episode's publication time, never before: a static feed has no clock. **Never put a real site, list, show, sender or address in `template/` or `examples/`** (D36): the template's examples use Harbour Lane Studio and `example.com`. **Media never edits a website repository or sends an email** (D55): a snippet goes to the author in chat.
